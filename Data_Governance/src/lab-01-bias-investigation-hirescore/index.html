Bias runs through the hiring pipeline, not just the model.

A structured audit of HireScore's ranking system — tracing how historical hiring patterns, skewed sampling, and proxy features combine to disadvantage women, graduates of non-elite universities, and candidates outside Accra and Ashanti.

01 — Executive Summary

Four bias types, one compounding effect

Each bias type reinforces the next: historical hiring outcomes shape the training set, sampling gaps concentrate it further, and proxy features let the model re-derive protected attributes it was never given directly.

02 — Training Data Composition

Where the imbalance enters the system

Illustrative breakdowns of the two imbalances named in the investigation: gender split in historical hires, and the job-category skew in the training sample.

Historical bias — training data reflects 72% male / 28% female hires, which the model learns as the "successful candidate" pattern.
Sampling bias — Software Engineering dominates the training set; other job categories are underrepresented relative to actual applicant volume.

05 — Mitigation Plan

A three-phase remediation path

This week

Immediate

  • Remove or down-weight university, location, LinkedIn connections
  • Introduce fairness-aware scoring thresholds
  • Track gender ratio, regional distribution, score gaps weekly
1 – 3 months

Short-term

  • Collect diverse, representative training data
  • Retrain with fairness-aware algorithms
  • Add manual review of top-ranked candidates
6 – 12 months

Long-term

  • Adopt Equal Opportunity as the primary fairness metric
  • Hybrid AI + human decision-making
  • Disclose AI use and allow candidate appeals
